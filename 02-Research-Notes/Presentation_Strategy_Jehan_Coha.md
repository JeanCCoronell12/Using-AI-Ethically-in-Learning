# Research Strategy & Presentation Proposal

## 📌 Context & Audience Engagement
Every student in the classroom has used AI for a writing task, a listening exercise, or homework. Therefore, the audience will not be bored; instead, they will immediately relate: *"Hey, that is me."*

With verified research data integrated into our structure, the professor will see that our findings are fully backed by academic studies.

---

## 📊 Core Research Data & Statistics

1. **High Student Adoption:**
   * Between May and December 2025, student AI usage for assignments surged from **48% to 62%**. Among university students, it has already reached **72%**. *(Source ID: 2308)*

2. **The Awareness Paradox:**
   * **67% of students believe** that over-relying on AI damages critical thinking. In other words, we use it even while knowing it can harm our cognitive development. *(Source ID: 2308)*

3. **Large-Scale Evidence:**
   * A *Science* journal study involving over **95,000 students** found that **1 out of 3** regularly uses AI for assignments, and **9%** use it to cheat. *(Source ID: 51bd)*

4. **UNESCO Guidelines:**
   * UNESCO states that AI must not replace human thinking; it must protect **"human agency"** and focus on teaching ethical usage rather than prohibiting the technology. *(Source ID: 8d7a)*

5. **Key Concept - "Cognitive Surrender":**
   * A 2026 Wharton study reveals that students accepted **FALSE AI responses 80% of the time**, displaying higher confidence than if they had solved the problem on their own. This directly mirrors our concept of the **"illusion of competence"**. *(Source ID: 6d38)*

## ⏱️ 3-Part Presentation Structure (3 to 10 Minutes)

### 👤 PERSONA 1 - Valentina Caldera (Core Idea + Research | ~3 min)
- **Hook Question:** *"Be honest: Have you ever used AI to do your homework?"*
- **Present Data:** 62% student adoption, 67% awareness of harm, 1 out of 3 users.
- **Core Thesis:** *"AI is not the problem. The problem is how we use it: as a tool or as a replacement."*

### 👤 PERSONA 2 - Jean Carlos Coronell (Analysis + Real-World Examples | ~3.5 min)
- **Distinction of Use:**
  * **ETHICAL USE:** Using AI for explanation, brainstorming, practicing listening, or grammar checking. You remain mentally active.
  * **UNETHICAL USE:** Copying and pasting full essays, or having AI complete listening modules for you. You learn nothing.
- **Real-World Evidence:** The Wharton study showing an 80% acceptance rate of false AI outputs (The illusion / "Espejismo").

### 👤 PERSONA 3 - Jehan Coha (Conclusion + The New Academic Honesty | ~3.5 min)
- **Proposed Solution:** *The New Academic Honesty* (Moving away from bans toward accountability):
  1. **Be transparent:** Acknowledge when and how AI was used.
  2. **Be critical:** Verify and cross-check all outputs.
  3. **Be the author:** You are the one who must learn; AI is only an assistant.
- **Closing Quote (Thought-Provoking):** *"AI can write your homework, but it cannot learn for you."*
